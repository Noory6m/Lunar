04 / CONSTRAINT-BASED DECISION SUPPORT

Find an operation window.

Set explicit requirements. Candidates use the same selected site and analyzed dates. No arbitrary safety score.

An interval is usable only when both endpoint states agree. Transition intervals and unknown geometry are excluded. Hidden events between samples remain possible. These are geometric requirements, not battery or radio-link guarantees.

Run an analysis to plan an operation.

Verification and height tests request fresh NASA geometry and require internet. Height tests use the original sampling interval and selected operation only; all scenarios must succeed before a stability conclusion is shown.

Decision method & evidence

Candidate starts are analyzed sample timestamps. Exact operation durations are clipped to intervals. Rank feasible candidates by the shortest maximum Earth gap, then longest joint-clear time, then earliest start. Unknown or transition intervals are not eligible. Results apply only within this terrain model.

5-minute verification re-queries the entire selected operation, not interpolated ephemerides. Compare coverage and acceptance at the two resolutions. A transition is reported as a time bracket, not an exact event time. Height sensitivity uses new Horizons coordinates at each height; it is not a statistical confidence interval or a DEM-accuracy study.

Local and regional grids cover finite areas. Positive elevation with no hit is model-clear, not proof of absence of distant terrain. No-hit at/below the reference horizon, incomplete validity masks and interval transitions are unresolved.

WHY THIS EXISTS

The sky can be clear.
The path may not be.

Near the lunar south pole, the Sun and Earth often sit low in the sky. Ridges and crater walls can interrupt solar access and direct Earth visibility. Lunar Horizon combines date-specific JPL geometry with a three-dimensional terrain model, so those hidden obstructions become visible.
